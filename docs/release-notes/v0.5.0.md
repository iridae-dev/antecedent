# Antecedent 0.5.0 — causal responses, not just contrasts

> Draft release notes. The package version has not been bumped and the release
> is not ready until the 0.5.0 release-validation gates pass.

Antecedent 0.5 makes a causal response a first-class object. Binary average
treatment effects remain unchanged, but continuous interventions no longer need
to masquerade as a sequence of unrelated binary contrasts.

```python
query = antecedent.ResponseCurve(
    "price",
    "demand",
    grid=[8.0, 9.0, 10.0, 11.0, 12.0],
)
result = antecedent.analyze(data, graph=dag, query=query)
```

The returned object separates four questions that were previously easy to
conflate:

- Was the response structurally identified?
- Is the requested treatment region empirically supported?
- Is the reported uncertainty pointwise, simultaneous, set-valued, or posterior?
- Which observation and modelling assumptions were actually used?

## What is new

The response query family includes mean curves, average derivatives, local
derivatives, elasticities, directional derivatives, and low-dimensional
Jacobians. Scalar query constructors follow the existing API convention:
`(treatment, outcome)` is positional and all scientific/numerical options are
keyword-only.

The initial complete-observation estimators are deliberately specific about
their contracts:

- Kennedy-style cross-fitted doubly robust pseudo-outcomes with local polynomial
  smoothing for continuous response curves;
- an observed-law Riesz average derivative estimator;
- model-dependent additive-GAM plug-in Jacobians and directional derivatives in
  at most two treatment dimensions.

Pointwise intervals are not labelled simultaneous. An opt-in Rust fixed-grid
band uses deterministic Rademacher wild-multiplier calibration conditional on
the cross-fitted pseudo-outcome and records that distinct provenance path.
Log-outcome derivative
transformations do not receive a partial delta-method interval when the required
covariance is unavailable. Requested grid points outside the observed treatment
range remain in the result but are marked as unsupported rather than silently
trimmed.

## Observation is not outcome

`antecedent.observation` describes complete, right/left/interval-censored,
truncated, and selected outcomes. The mechanism never implies its identifying
assumption. Independent censoring, outcome-independent selection, and structural
Gaussian likelihood assumptions must be supplied explicitly.

Implemented estimator primitives include selected-outcome logistic IPW/AIPW,
unconditional Kaplan–Meier IPCW (including delayed entry for right censoring),
left-censoring sign reversal, and an explicit opt-in Gaussian likelihood for
censored/truncated observations. Unsupported conditional censoring and
mechanism/assumption combinations fail closed.

Scalar `ResponseCurve` execution composes selected-outcome IPW/AIPW and marginal
right/left-censoring IPCW into point estimation when the declared assumptions
license that composition. For selection, `OutcomeIndependentGiven` must include
the treatment and every graph-licensed adjustment variable. The result reports
`uncertainty.kind == "none"` with a structured warning: complete-data Kennedy
bands do not include uncertainty from the fitted observation mechanism and are
therefore not reused. Interval censoring and truncation remain on the explicit
Gaussian observation-likelihood stage; 0.5.0 does not present that likelihood
as a causal-response MLE.

## Bounds, transport, and interference

The result vocabulary can hold scalar identified intervals and function-valued
response envelopes. Sharp binary-IV Balke–Pearl bounds on `E[Y(1)-Y(0)]` are
available in Rust and as `antecedent.identify.binary_iv_bounds` (response-type
enumeration against the observed law). That is a contrast bound, not a
continuous-response curve estimator.

Structural transport is separate from the existing prior/evidence-transfer
machinery. Version 0.5 introduces single-source selection diagrams, certified
direct and singleton-component transport cases, and trial-to-target binary
randomized IPW/AIPW. General multi-node sID cases outside the implemented subset
return `NotCertified`; they are not falsely labelled non-transportable.

Randomized interference uses the decomposition from the literature: assignment
design, exposure mapping, then estimand. Bernoulli, complete, and cluster
randomization can be combined with own-treatment, neighbor count/fraction, and
weighted-neighbor exposures. Exposure probabilities are exact through the
enumeration limit and deterministically simulated beyond it; Horvitz–Thompson
and Hájek contrasts remain separately reported.

## Provenance and parity

Every substantive 0.5 algorithm has a machine-readable record under
[`provenance/`](../../provenance/) naming the primary paper and implemented
section. External packages are used only as pinned black-box oracles: no upstream
source or translated tests enter this repository. The response parity inventory
records candidate oracles honestly; a candidate is not called a comparison until
its frozen reference command and outputs exist.

## Deliberate limits

- Exact continuous point responses are not promised universal root-n inference.
- Non-parametric response surfaces are limited to low treatment dimension.
- Multi-source meta-transport and cyclic/equilibrium causal systems remain out
  of scope for 0.5.
- Package version is **0.5.0** (workspace + Python).
