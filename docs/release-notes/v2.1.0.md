# Antecedent 2.1.0 release notes

2.1.0 is in development. This branch expands Bayesian estimator scope and the evidence behind its support claims. The [support matrix](../support-matrix.md) remains the authoritative coordinate license.

## Randomized experiment and provider contracts

- `experiment.ExperimentDesign` and `experiment.RandomizedEffect` now run through graphless `prepare` / `analyze` as a distinct native randomized query for Bernoulli, complete, stratified, cluster, and fixed-cell 2×2 factorial assignment. The frozen query and result retain assignment probabilities, block allocations, cluster or unit IDs, and arm labels. Bernoulli uses Horvitz–Thompson; complete and stratified designs use difference-in-means estimators with design-based variance estimates. Cluster assignment uses unit-weighted cluster totals and a conservative cluster-level Neyman variance. Fixed-cell factorial analysis requires at least two units in each of four cells and reports both marginal main effects and their interaction with separate conservative cellwise variances. `SwitchbackEffect` also runs through the retained randomized path with sequence and period identity, known marginal assignment probabilities, and an independent-sequence sandwich variance under no carryover and no between-sequence interference. `experiment.ComplierEffect` carries observed treatment receipt through the Bernoulli study path and reports outcome ITT, positive receipt first stage, Wald CACE/LATE, and an independent-unit influence variance under exclusion and monotonicity. These retained experiment queries report no confidence interval and remain off the support-matrix axis. `MultiArmExperimentDesign` now carries independent three-or-more-action assignment through the same retained `RandomizedEffect` path. The direct utility and Study route share the native Horvitz–Thompson arm-mean estimator. The frozen result reports every action contrast to the first arm with covariance-free variance bounds, observed support counts, and no interval or support-matrix license. Missing action support and unsupported adjustment combinations refuse.
- `factorial.estimate` adds native 2×2 Bernoulli factorial cell means, main effects, interaction, and covariance-free variance bounds. It does not yet cover block, switchback, or multi-arm factorial designs, confidence intervals, or a licensed support coordinate.
- `RandomizedEffect(..., ancova_covariates=("baseline_a", "baseline_b"))` fits multi-covariate ANCOVA within the retained graphless randomized Study for independent Bernoulli assignment with a common probability. The direct utility and retained route share the native OLS kernel. Query/artifact identity carries the covariates; the result reports a treatment coefficient and labeled independent-row HC0 variance, without a calibrated interval or support-matrix license. Incompatible designs, CUPED and receipt combinations, duplicate or rank-deficient covariates refuse.
- `extensibility.CausalProviderSpec` describes external provider contracts. Python providers can be explicitly registered or loaded from a separately installed `antecedent.providers` entry-point factory with `providers.load_entry_point(name)`, without rebuilding Antecedent or Rust. Loading is explicit; package import does not scan or execute plugins. `analyze(data, query=ProviderQuery(name, request))` dispatches to that registry and returns a structured `ProviderAnalysisResult` with the output, uncertainty semantics, provenance, trust boundary, and optional artifact. It does not recast arbitrary plugin output as a native point or interval claim. Python providers cannot claim native licensing. `handoff.EconMLSpec.as_provider(executor)` adapts a caller-owned fit callback; it uses the existing `.attach()` artifact encoder and returns its bytes through the provider result. The original `econml()` and `.attach()` APIs and artifact round-trip remain unchanged.
- `ProviderRegistry.verify` now runs independent caller-supplied `ProviderVerificationFixture` cases through the registered provider, checking known-truth output, uncertainty, assumptions, support, provenance, artifact digest and optional decode, plus deterministic replay when declared. Passing all cases records a digest-bearing `ProviderVerificationReport` and promotes the provider to `verified_extension` without a Python or Rust rebuild. Fixture truth remains caller-supplied; this bounded gate does not grant native licensing or interval calibration.
- `policy.BinaryPolicy` and `policy.MultiActionPolicy` add precomputed binary or multi-action recommendations and deterministic score ranking. Native Rust evaluates policy/reference values using known randomized action probabilities, checks availability, per-action capacity and budget, and accounts for action costs. The caller must hold evaluation rows out from policy selection. `MultiActionPolicyValue(..., baseline_groups=...)` now also reports retained action-versus-control CATE points by pre-treatment group, requiring observed control and each action in every group. These routes do not train policies or CATE models, certify the split, or publish conditional intervals or a support-matrix license.
- Retained fixed-policy evaluation now exposes paired pointwise 95% policy-value and incremental-value intervals for independent randomized evaluation subjects with known propensities and sufficient assignment matches. Binary IPW requires at least 120 subjects; held-out AIPW and multi-action IPW require at least 300. Cross-fitted nuisance predictions and potentially binding global capacity or budget selection remain point-only. The 2,000-replicate calibration tests measure binary IPW at 90% and 95% and held-out AIPW and costed multi-action IPW at 95%; a 120-subject multi-action probe undercovered, so it is excluded. The interval path remains off-axis in the support matrix and does not license a support cell.
- `policy.ConditionalDoseResponse` now enters graphless `prepare` / `analyze` with frozen baseline groups, target doses, bandwidth, local-support threshold, and declared density provenance. The retained route and direct utility share one native triangular-kernel inverse-density estimator. Known-truth, positivity refusal, refresh row-binding, Python parity, and artifact round-trip fixtures pass. Every group-target point reports support diagnostics and descriptive local SD; no policy value, standard error, interval, or support-matrix license is claimed.
- `regimes.evaluate_regime_value` adds a native point-only sequential inverse-probability evaluator for prespecified static or history-adaptive binary treatment regimes. The caller supplies treatment and censoring probabilities; the result includes trajectory support and weight diagnostics. It does not fit nuisance models, cross-fit, or report uncertainty, and it does not add a support-matrix license.
- `regimes.LongitudinalRegimeQuery` carries prespecified subject histories and known sequential randomization probabilities through retained graphless `prepare` / `analyze`. Static or subject-specific binary actions, censoring, subject IDs, and fold ownership are frozen with the study. The retained route accepts `method="g_formula"` with caller-supplied subject-period conditional reward predictions and `method="sequential_dr"` with caller-supplied Q predictions, aligned subject/prediction fold IDs, monotone observation histories, and conditional censoring probabilities. The latter shares its native recursive estimator with the direct utility and retains support-weight diagnostics in artifacts. It reports a regime value without a treatment-effect placeholder or interval. Antecedent does not fit or verify nuisance models or actual out-of-fold training; caller-estimated probabilities remain outside this retained route, and it adds no support-matrix license.
- `LongitudinalRegimeQuery.from_dynamic_rule(...)` materializes a history-adaptive binary callback from each subject's past observed actions and predecision covariates, then runs the existing retained Rust Study estimator. The caller supplies a stable rule ID, version, and provenance, which are preserved in query and result artifacts with the resolved actions. The callback is caller code and cannot be replayed from an artifact. This route preserves subject folds, censoring and positivity checks, and point-only uncertainty; it adds no support-matrix license.
- `regimes.evaluate_sequential_gformula` adds a plug-in regime value from caller-supplied period-specific conditional rewards. It preserves unique subject IDs and fold ownership in provenance and checks treatment/censoring positivity. It does not fit or cross-fit the outcome model.
- `regimes.evaluate_sequential_doubly_robust` adds a native backward-recursive augmented score with caller-supplied Q predictions and treatment/censoring probabilities. It checks subject and prediction fold-ID alignment and monotone dropout, but cannot verify that models were trained out of fold. It returns a point value without an interval.
- `regimes.fit_marginal_structural_model` adds a native additive terminal-outcome MSM with caller-supplied stabilized treatment probabilities, optional censoring weights, and subject-clustered CR1 standard errors. `LongitudinalRegimeQuery.marginal_structural_model(...)` now runs the same kernel through retained `prepare` / `analyze`, preserving subject folds, nuisance inputs, period coefficients, pointwise standard errors, and query/result artifact identity. Known-truth, refusal, artifact, and direct-versus-retained Python fixtures pass. The path does not verify out-of-fold nuisance fitting, reports no interval or calibration claim, and adds no support-matrix license.
- `quasi.DifferenceInDifferences` adds a native repeated-cross-section 2×2 DiD point utility. It reports the parallel untreated trends, no anticipation, stable assignment, and no interference assumptions. It does not license panel/staggered designs, event studies, clustered uncertainty, or any support-matrix cell.
- `quasi.PanelDifferenceInDifferences` adds retained `prepare` / `analyze` for balanced two-period panels and, through `repeated_cross_section(...)`, independent repeated cross sections. Both report pointwise cluster-robust standard errors or an optional higher-level cluster. The panel requires one pre/post pair per subject and stable treatment within subject; the repeated cross section requires one row per subject. Neither reports a p-value or interval or adds a support-matrix license.
- `quasi.AugmentedPanelDiD` now enters the same retained `prepare` / `analyze` family with one row per subject and supplied propensity and untreated-change predictions. The direct utility and Study share the native ATT estimator. Subject, cluster, treatment, and nuisance-column identity are retained; strict propensity overlap, duplicate subjects, and changed prepared row order are refused. The result and artifact preserve overlap diagnostics and the caller's cross-fitting declaration. The artifact explicitly omits a standard error, and neither route reports an interval or adds a support-matrix license. Native known-truth/refusal/artifact and public Python workflow fixtures pass.
- `quasi.StaggeredAdoption` adds balanced-panel group-time ATT contrasts against cohort-0 never-treated controls, with cohort/period subject counts as descriptive support diagnostics. `StaggeredAdoption(..., event_study=True)` now retains the full cohort-specific event-time curve through native `Study`, Python `prepare` / `analyze`, and artifacts; the direct event-study utility shares the native estimator. Both routes report pointwise cluster-robust standard errors using subject IDs by default or an optional higher-level cluster column, with the finite-cluster multiplier G/(G-1). Event-study contrasts omit event time -1; pre-adoption contrasts are descriptive and are not a pretrend test. The top-level scalar on the retained event study is its first post-adoption cohort/period contrast; the full curve is in `result.panel_did.effects`. Both assume cohort-specific parallel untreated trends, no anticipation, absorbing treatment, valid never-treated controls, independent clusters, and no interference. They report no p-values or intervals and add no support-matrix license.
- `quasi.SyntheticControl` and `quasi.SyntheticDifferenceInDifferences` now enter graphless `prepare` / `analyze` with frozen unit and period identity. The native route fits convex donor weights for synthetic control, or donor and pre-period weights for synthetic DiD, and preserves fit diagnostics in distinct result and artifact sections. Known-truth native and public Python fixtures pass, with explicit refusals for unsupported panel support and changed prepared row order. Both remain point-only and off the support-matrix axis; placebo ranks from synthetic control are descriptive and uncalibrated.
- Synthetic control can additionally declare uniform assignment of exactly one treated unit. The retained native path enumerates every candidate unit under the Fisher sharp null and reports an exact two-sided assignment p-value alongside the full statistic distribution. The donor-only placebo rank remains descriptive; the effect has no interval and this path remains off the support-matrix axis.
- `SyntheticControl(augmentation_ridge=...)` adds a retained donor-trained ridge outcome-model correction to the simplex gap. The query, native estimate, Python result, and artifact preserve the penalty, unadjusted gap, correction, and adjusted effect. Known-truth outside-hull, refusal, and artifact-forgery fixtures pass across the native and public Python paths. The additional donor-model transport assumption is reported; the adjusted effect is point-only and remains off the support-matrix axis. Augmentation refuses synthetic DiD and exact unit-randomization combinations.
- `quasi.FuzzyRegressionDiscontinuity` and `quasi.RegressionKink` enter graphless retained `prepare` / `analyze` as well as direct utilities. The native local-polynomial kernel reports the point, first stage, local support, joint HC0 component errors, and a pointwise 95% normal interval for a positive finite ratio SE after weak-stage screening. A cubic pilot corrects the jump; a quartic pilot corrects the kink, after a wide-bandwidth quartic-trend stress fixture exposed undercoverage with the cubic pilot. Known-truth calibration covers 1,898/2,000 fuzzy-jump draws and 1,906/2,000 kink draws, including the quartic trend, at nominal 95%. Python and result artifacts retain the interval. These routes remain outside the geometric support matrix, and arbitrary bandwidth choices, weak-instrument-robust inference, and running-variable manipulation diagnostics are not licensed by those fixtures.
- `survival.SurvivalOutcome` and `survival.CompetingRisksOutcome` run through retained `prepare` / `analyze` with explicit individual randomization and marginal `IndependentGiven(())` censoring/entry assumptions. Known conditional censoring survival can be supplied as aligned data columns through `KnownCensoringSurvival`, using the shared native IPCW estimator in the retained query, result, and artifact flow. Explicit `bootstrap` adds arm-stratified subject-resampling pointwise 95% percentile intervals for RMST and fixed-horizon survival or target-cause incidence contrasts. Repeated-sampling native gates cover uncensored survival (400 studies), fixed known-G survival (240), and fixed known-G competing risks at two event times (240), each requiring at least 90% observed coverage. Full curves have no simultaneous bands; delayed-entry intervals are refused. Supplied censoring functions are held fixed, not fitted or verified. Survival remains outside the licensed support-matrix axes.
- `InterferenceQuery` now retains a second frequentist construction for completely randomized clusters with an explicit matching partial-interference partition, within-cluster network, `NeighborFraction`, and the total exposure contrast `(0, 0) → (1, 1)`. It reports a cluster-level conservative variance and no interval. The existing Bernoulli/`NeighborCount` construction is the only licensed and calibrated frequentist cell; the cluster construction has no transferred calibration claim.
- `InterferenceQuery` also retains exact two-stage saturation contrasts: clusters are completely assigned low/high treatment probabilities, then units are independently Bernoulli assigned within clusters. A fixed within-cluster network supports direct, spillover, and total exposure contrasts with `NeighborCount`, `NeighborFraction`, or `WeightedNeighborExposure`. The direct Python utility now shares the native estimator with `prepare` / `analyze`. Known-truth, refusal, artifact, and public Python parity checks pass. This construction remains off-axis and point-only; its covariance-free variance proxy is descriptive and no interval calibration or license is transferred from the Bernoulli cell.
- `InterferenceQuery` now also retains observational network-exposure contrasts under an explicit no-unmeasured-network-confounding assumption. Callers supply the fixed within-cluster network, partial-interference partition, observed assignment, both exposure propensities, and whether those propensities are known or externally estimated. Neighbor count, fraction, and weighted exposure mappings share one native estimator across the direct utility and `prepare` / `analyze`; exposed-unit and cluster support, probability bounds, and descriptive cluster-robust variance are retained in the result and artifact. Known-truth, refusal, tampering, and public Python parity checks pass. The route is off-axis and point-only: the library does not verify propensity fitting or exchangeability and reports no licensed interval.

## Evidence and assumptions slice

- Single-source z-transport covers finite discrete diagrams of up to twelve observed and four controllable variables. A positive result is a checked TRz formula and evaluates from the joints that formula cites, including the registered four-variable surrogate and an admissible selection diagram. A line-11 certificate is structural in the declared controllable set: the same terminal is reached with an empty catalog as with the full experiment family. Two sources are searched separately; one identifying source is enough, both line-11 terminals are one obstruction, and complementary factors combine under two registered factorizations: two disconnected graph components, or one connected graph whose intervened outcomes m-separate into two groups given the treatments, with each factor bound to a single source under the joint-regime rule. A single connected c-factor that would need one fabricated joint over both sources' interventions is refused as `z_transport.multi_source_combination_not_searched`. Empirical cited tables publish an equal-tail percentile bootstrap by default, and the same tables also accept the empirical-support Bayesian bootstrap and the declared-state-space Dirichlet posterior. A published interval is pointwise, withholds when the bootstrap failure rule trips, and its reason is `estimator_grid_not_measured`; exact laws stay point-only, and that interval is not a coverage claim. The result stays sound and incomplete. Outcome-kernel sensitivity stays on the registered surrogate factorization.
- Checked transport proofs expose factor leaves, their source regimes, and binding failures. Immutable hypothetical catalog deltas let a planner test proposed evidence without adding it to a prepared analysis.
- A finite discrete outcome-kernel contamination model reports an exact assumption range, extremal witnesses, and decision tipping point. Its range is not a confidence interval.
- A finite candidate planner tests hypothetical evidence deltas, ranks only checked sufficient additions by declared cost, and emits a replayable proposal with a base-catalog digest. Declared feasibility is supplied by the planner caller; a cost rank is not a success probability.
- A fixed three-node Markovian mediation DAG has a point-only natural direct-effect route with coupled exogenous draws and typed incompatible-world refusals. See the [counterfactual coverage matrix](../../parity/counterfactual_coverage.md).
- A CPU baseline measures end-to-end cross-fitted neural nuisance fitting, including fold planning. No GPU provider is introduced.
- The [transport coverage matrix](../../parity/transport_coverage.md) and [calibration backlog](../../parity/calibration_backlog.md) expose licensed and refused scope and unmeasured intervals.

## Bayesian estimator changes

- Bayesian conditional effects accept multiple declared modifiers and evaluate interactions within each posterior draw. Non-Gaussian conditional and response levels are evaluated through their inverse link on the outcome scale.
- Response curves can report a simultaneous band from the same joint posterior draws on a fixed grid.
- Bayesian anomaly and change attribution preserve shared row-law draws across components. Trial-to-target transport uses a Bayesian bootstrap over observed trial rows, conditional on supplied selection and treatment probabilities. Finite-network interference has a separate Gaussian model-based posterior route.
- CPDAG and PAG graph-posterior intervention responses run cheap and full validation while retaining graph mass, unresolved atoms, and atom-level failures.
- Finite-discrete structural transport offers separate empirical-support bootstrap and declared-state-space Dirichlet law providers. An exact sparse-table Dirichlet moment check and public Python artifact lifecycle test exercise both providers. A published equal-tail interval is pointwise, shares one draw sequence across a treatment grid, and withholds that interval when the bootstrap failure rule trips. Its coverage reason remains `estimator_grid_not_measured` until the release calibration pass.
- `bayesian.basis.gcomp` fits a Gaussian quadratic basis with treatment interactions and a declared shrinkage prior for `AllObserved` ATE and row CATE. A synthetic 100-replicate nominal 90% ATE check covered 95/100; no registry coverage record is attached yet.
- `bayesian.robust_ate` refits cross-fitted weighted outcome and propensity nuisances under shared Bayesian-bootstrap row weights. Its interval is labeled a modular bootstrap pushforward. Four bounded 40-replicate scenarios test single-nuisance, joint misspecification, and weak-overlap behavior; nominal 90% interval coverage ranged from 77.5% to 90%, so a calibrated coverage claim is withheld.
- `rd.bayesian_local_linear` has a dedicated sharp RD model conditional on the declared bandwidth; a 300-replicate exact-law check covered 95.7% at nominal 95%. `iv.bayesian_joint_linear` uses a joint Gaussian structural model with a fixed unit disturbance loading and known unit stage variances. Its 300-replicate exact-law interval check passes; the earlier free-loading control-function candidate covered 100% and is not licensed.

## Explicit refusals

The new licenses are limited to their stated outcome models, populations, exposure mappings, graph classes, and validation tiers. Unlisted coordinates and unsupported priors or target populations still refuse. See the [support matrix](../support-matrix.md#refusal-reasons) for coordinate-level reasons.
