# Transport limits by route

Each transport route answers one family of questions under a stated theorem scope and size limits. This page says, route by route, what it decides, what it leaves undecided and the bounds it runs within. Start with [Carry an effect to another population](transport/carry-an-effect.md) for the task; come here when a route refuses or you need to know whether your question fits one.

Two ideas recur.

- **Completeness is per family.** A route that is *sound and incomplete* returns a formula only when its checks pass. When it returns `not_certified`, that says the search did not find a formula, not that none exists ([when transport fails](transport-failure.md)). Only the two classical identifiers are complete, and only inside their published experimental-information families.
- **Most routes return a point.** A route either evaluates exact supplied laws or an empirical plug-in of counted laws and states whether an interval exists. Every artifact a route exports is replayed by its consumer under the producer's stored limits. A digest is an integrity check, not authentication: replay detects corruption and edits that change the answer, and cannot detect a wrong graph or law the producer sealed deliberately.

The numeric bounds below are the declared implementation limits; the full list is in [supported input bounds](../supported-bounds.md). Requests beyond a bound refuse with a typed reason rather than being truncated.

## Finite scenario envelopes

`prepare_transport_scenarios` takes a `TransportScenarioSet`: a finite list you supply of named fixed graphs over the same variables, each with its own selection targets and optionally a declared weight, plus one shared coordinate schema (names, domains, cardinalities, units). A disagreement between scenarios, or a catalog, law, sample or request outside the schema, refuses with `schema_mismatch` (`scenarios.coordinate_mismatch`). Values must be finite numbers inside the declared domain; units are compared only between the schema and the catalog's environments, and only when both declare one.

Each scenario is decided by the classical catalog sID route with that route's scope. The number of observed variables is bounded only by the search budget, and a stop leaves the remaining scenarios `unevaluated` with a receipt. A tight budget can add an inconclusive obligation to a result but cannot by itself produce `not_certified` on this route; `not_certified` is reachable on a conditional scenario (below).

**Conditional questions.** With `conditioned_on` (Rust `StudyBuilder::conditional_transport_scenarios`) every scenario asks `P*(y | do(x), w)` and is decided by [ADMG conditional transport](#admg-conditional-transport) with that route's scope: at most {{bound:transport.admg_conditional.max_observed}} observed variables, 0-{{bound:transport.admg_conditional.max_treatments}} treatments and 1-{{bound:transport.admg_conditional.max_conditioned}} conditioned variables, otherwise refused for the whole set with `route_not_supported` (`admg_transport.bounds_exceeded`). A reduced joint with a verified s-hedge is reported `not_certified` with an inspection-only obstruction candidate; a scenario is `proven_non_transportable` only when an exactly verified two-model witness exists. A zero-mass conditioning event is that scenario's `support_failure`. Exact laws only: counted laws refuse with `cell_not_licensed` (`admg_transport.interval_withheld`).

**What the envelope is.**

- A per-scenario decision. Each scenario binds its own evidence and is reported whatever its status: `identified`, `structurally_unidentified`, `missing_evidence`, `not_certified`, `unsupported_provider`, `support_failure` or `unevaluated`.
- A structural envelope: the minimum and maximum of the target response over the scenarios that identified, naming the scenarios that attain them. It is not a confidence interval or a sharp bound and says nothing about scenarios that did not identify.
- With declared weights, a report that never renormalizes over the identified scenarios; the other scenarios' weights and the undeclared residual stay unaccounted mass.
- Points from supplied exact laws or from empirical plug-in tables fitted once from `StatisticalTransportData`; never an interval (the bootstrap is fixed at zero replicates).
- Bounded by one shared search budget (`max_steps`, `max_depth`, `memory_bytes`, `cancel`). A 65th scenario refuses with `route_not_supported` (`scenarios.count`).

**What it is not.** Not an equivalence-class solver: a `Cpdag` or `Pag` refuses with `scenarios.equivalence_class_input`; use [discovered-structure transport](discovered-structure-transport.md). Not cross-scenario inference: `aggregate_interval()` always refuses with `scenario_aggregate_not_licensed`.

`consume_transport_scenarios_artifact` replays the whole set under the producer's recorded budget and requires an identical report. A premises digest binds scenarios, weights, schema, question, request and budgets; a separate data-identity digest binds the evidence catalog, stored laws, provider identity and fitted-sample summaries, so refreshed data replaces only the second. A report truncated by an operation, depth or memory bound exports and replays the identical prefix; one truncated by cancellation is never exported.

## Decision inspection and mechanism sensitivity on the z route

Every bounded single-source decision (`ZTransportStage.decide`) exposes structured inspection. `identified` carries the checked proof graph and its per-factor binding obligations; `missing_evidence` names the unbound factor; `proven_non_transportable` carries the reduced line-11 terminal record; `not_certified` carries a typed reason and the recursive rules the search explored. A budget or cancellation is `outcome = "exhausted"` with a receipt of the limits in force and what was consumed.

`PreparedZTransportStage.mechanism_sensitivity` applies only to the registered surrogate formula (an outcome conditional times its shared parent marginal from one cited `do(z)` joint); any other in-bound graph refuses sensitivity with `IncompatibleFormula` and the point result is unaffected. The sensitivity range is an exact assumption range, not a sampling interval.

`antecedent.transport.advanced.joint_mechanism_sensitivity(stage, JointDeviation(...))` perturbs the outcome kernel and the shared parent marginal together, each within its own fraction bound (a box; a coupled total budget refuses with `joint_sensitivity.budget_coupling`). It reports the exact joint range, axis tipping points and a tipping frontier of bisection brackets. Fixed-graph parent perturbations, the treatment mechanism, more than two factors and source-target discrepancy diagnostics refuse with `joint_sensitivity.*` details. Bounds: at most {{bound:sensitivity.joint_mechanism.max_factors}} factors (the outcome kernel and the shared parent marginal), {{bound:sensitivity.joint_mechanism.max_parent_levels}} parent levels, {{bound:sensitivity.joint_mechanism.max_outcome_categories}} outcome categories, a frontier grid of 1 to {{bound:sensitivity.joint_mechanism.max_frontier_points}} points, at most {{bound:sensitivity.joint_mechanism.max_operations}} search operations, a bisection depth of {{bound:sensitivity.joint_mechanism.max_bisection_iterations}} iterations per frontier line and a memory cap of at most 512 MiB. The range is never a confidence interval and covers only the declared contamination class. A separate promoted endpoint-bootstrap route has an internal bootstrap request cap of {{bound:sensitivity.joint_uncertainty.max_bootstrap_internal}}; its interval is conditional on the measured construction. Derivation and scope: [joint mechanism sensitivity](joint-mechanism-sensitivity.md).

## Learned continuous-outcome transport

One cell: a randomized binary source treatment with known probabilities, a continuous outcome, complete baseline covariates equal to the certified standardizers (a direct or baseline-standardization certificate), and an overlap-supported target. The estimand is `E_target[E(Y | X, A=1, S=1) - E(Y | X, A=0, S=1)]`.

After `advanced.prepare_learned_continuous(query, data)`, use `prepared.estimate()` for a point or `prepared.interval()` for the pointwise analytic interval; `prepared.export()` saves the most recent result and `advanced.consume_learned_continuous(artifact)` replays it without refitting the learners.

- **Target and design.** A nested cohort (the target is the nonparticipants of one IID cohort) or independent samples (a separately sampled representative IID target with fixed sizes). These are distinct declared designs; non-IID sampling refuses (`learned_transport.non_iid_design`).
- **Nuisances.** Outcome regressions per arm and source membership are `antecedent.learners` specs, cross-fitted on one shared stratified fold assignment that depends on source and arm flags only. Provider implementation, version and the fold assignment are recorded.
- **Claim.** Model double robustness of the point: consistent when either the outcome regressions or the participation model is. No efficiency, rate or CATE claim; a heterogeneous or simultaneous target refuses (`learned_transport.cate_requested`).
- **Overlap.** Source-membership and treatment overlap are separate diagnostics with separate thresholds; insufficient overlap refuses (`learned_transport.membership_overlap`, `learned_transport.treatment_overlap`) rather than extrapolating.
- **Estimator menu.** `advanced.estimator_menu(query, outcome=..., membership=...)` lists, without fitting, which estimators are eligible for this graph, query and learners, with their required laws, nuisance tasks, support, sampling design, uncertainty status and refusal. Selection stays manual; nothing is recommended.
- **Bounds.** At most {{bound:transport.learned_continuous_trial.max_folds}} cross-fitting folds and a bootstrap cap of {{bound:transport.learned_continuous_trial.max_bootstrap}} replicates (floor {{bound:transport.learned_continuous_trial.min_bootstrap}}); a consumer admits at most {{bound:transport.learned_continuous_trial.max_rows_consumed}} rows and {{bound:transport.learned_continuous_trial.max_features_consumed}} features.
- **Interval.** `prepared.interval()` with `bootstrap=0` gives the pointwise analytic influence interval and its standard error, using a design-specific variance (a random target count for a nested cohort; separate target and trial contributions for independent samples). The band is `psi ± z √V`. It assumes IID sampling, positivity, correct identification and the usual cross-fitting product-rate condition; it is not simultaneous or a CATE interval. Requesting the closed outer-refit percentile bootstrap withholds the interval. Read `result.inspect().calibration` for whether a coverage record is bound.
- **Artifact consumer.** Replays the certificate, folds and score; it does not replay the nuisance fits, so stored out-of-fold predictions and provider versions are producer evidence.

## Two-step temporal sequences

One cell: a fixed two-step intervention sequence `[a1, a2]` over one discrete action alphabet (at most {{bound:transport.two_step_temporal.max_actions}} actions), on an explicit two-slice unrolled selection diagram with at most {{bound:transport.two_step_temporal.max_history_states}} complete covariate histories. The estimand is the target distribution and mean of the outcome under `do(A_1=a1, A_2=a2)`. The claim is exact and point-only; temporal sampling intervals come from the [measured temporal adapter](../population-time-uncertainty.md), and this exact-law route refuses an interval request (`temporal_transport.interval_requested`).

- **Unrolling.** `TemporalSequenceSpec` places every coordinate in time: baseline covariates, covariates observed before each action, the two actions and the outcome. Edges may not run backward in time; latent confounding is a bidirected edge. `antecedent.transport.advanced.unroll_two_slice` builds the diagram from a lagged template; the initial state is fixed, not modelled.
- **One longitudinal intervention.** Both actions are the treatment set of one classical question. The two time steps are never transported separately and multiplied, so a time-varying confounder is handled by the joint identification.
- **Mechanism differences.** A selection target on a coordinate is a time-indexed difference at that slice; every other non-action coordinate is assumed invariant, and the report lists each assumption per slice. An action cannot be a selection target.
- **Sequence semantics.** The sequence is hard: one fixed action per step, never a policy reading the history. `[a, b]` and `[b, a]` are different interventions.
- **Evidence and support.** The report lists, per slice, the regimes the derivation actually cites. Every initial state and complete history appears with its target mass and status; a history is outside support unless the supplied laws serve every source leaf the proof cites there. A first action the target never takes at a reached state refuses (`temporal_transport.history_outside_support`) rather than extrapolating.
- **Budget.** One `SearchBudget` covers identification, verification replays and growth of the history lattice. A stop is a receipt (`temporal_transport.history_budget`), never a non-identification verdict; caps refuse as `temporal_transport.bounds_exceeded`.
- **Refresh and horizon.** A same-window evidence refresh re-estimates under the same proof; the target's initial-state masses may not move (a moved initial state is a new period). The licensed horizon is {{bound:transport.two_step_temporal.horizon}} steps; a longer horizon, a third period or another window refuses (`temporal_transport.horizon`) and needs a new preparation. See also the [temporal recalculation workflow](../recalculation-capabilities.md).

## Multi-source limited experiments

`identify_multi_source_z_transport` (Python) and `decide_mz_transport` (Rust) answer a target effect from two to {{bound:transport.multi_source_limited_experiment.max_sources}} source populations, each with its own selection targets, controllable set and experiment levels (Bareinboim, Lee, Honavar and Pearl, NeurIPS 2013). The result is sound and incomplete within the bounds ({{bound:transport.multi_source_limited_experiment.max_controllable_per_source}} controllables per source, {{bound:transport.multi_source_limited_experiment.max_candidate_regimes}} candidate regimes, {{bound:transport.multi_source_limited_experiment.max_search_states}} operations, depth {{bound:transport.multi_source_limited_experiment.max_depth}}) and never mixes sources inside one c-factor.

Where this search departs from the published algorithm:

- A checked obstruction is certified only at a forced terminal with no active experiment where no source can exchange. A published non-transportable case reached after an exchange is `not_certified` (`mz_transport.fabricated_joint` or `mz_transport.search_incomplete`), never an obstruction.
- The published weighted combination over all certifying sources is replaced by the first certifying source in canonical order; a missing regime of that source is reported even when another source could supply it. Either source gives the same number.
- Experiments on the target population are supported by the paper and refused here (`invalid_argument`, `mz_transport.invalid_catalog`).

One `SearchBudget` covers target-only, each source's z decision, the combined search and the obstruction replay; an identified decision carries a success-path receipt that a consumer replays and must reproduce. Refusals pair a reason code with an `mz_transport.*` detail: `route_not_supported` (`bounds_exceeded`), `invalid_argument` (`invalid_query`, `invalid_catalog`, `functional_graph_mismatch`), `transport_not_certified`, `transport_missing_evidence` (`missing_joint_regime`), `transport_proven_non_transportable` (`checked_obstruction`), `transport_budget_cancel` and `transport_missing_provider` (`empirical_counts_required`).

Points are exact or the empirical plug-in of counted laws. The joint-bootstrap interval route is closed (`cell_not_licensed`, `mz_transport.interval_withheld`): no public constructor yields an interval and every public consumer refuses an artifact that carries one.

## Mixed-source proof search

`identify_mixed_source_transport` (Python) and `decide_mixed_source` (Rust) answer `P(y | do(x))` in one population from several studies of it, each measuring a different set of variables or experimenting on different ones. The theorem-scoped routes run first (target-first sID, then the declared z or mz route, then the classical meta route); a solved query returns `named_route`. Otherwise a generic search chains a frozen rule set (input, marginalize, condition, product and do-calculus rules 1 to 3, each with at most {{bound:identify.mixed_source_proof_search.max_move}} moved variables and at most {{bound:identify.mixed_source_proof_search.max_intervention_set}} intervened variables) over the target population's whole-population, available, unconditioned joint laws. Every step records its premises and the study distribution it uses, and an independent proof checker replays each one.

Bounds: at most {{bound:identify.mixed_source_proof_search.max_observed}} observed variables, {{bound:identify.mixed_source_proof_search.max_distributions}} usable distributions and {{bound:identify.mixed_source_proof_search.max_sources}} sources, under a search budget of {{bound:identify.mixed_source_proof_search.max_search_states}} operations at depth {{bound:identify.mixed_source_proof_search.max_depth}}.

**How incomplete.** The search is sound and incomplete. Compared with the complete Shpitser-Pearl ID algorithm on a single observational study, it never identified a query ID refutes, and it identifies every query on three-node graphs and nearly all on four- and five-node graphs; it misses the napkin family (where ID's formula is a ratio of a marginalized c-factor), every query with more than three treatments, and many identifiable queries at six nodes where the operation budget runs out. A `not_certified` or `exhausted` answer is therefore never evidence of non-identification; the named sID route runs first and decides those cases exactly. For several studies there is no complete oracle here, so the claim is soundness against enumerated structural models with no completeness figure.

Outcomes: `identified`, `named_route`, `missing_evidence` (`mixed_search.missing_joint`: the exact joint a study holds only as separate marginals), `not_certified` (the rule set reached its fixpoint) and `exhausted` (limits receipt). Regimes of other populations, selected samples, model artifacts, conditioned projections and value-restricted experiments are excluded and listed with their reason: a trial of `do(X = true)` supplies only one level, while the search states identification for every level. The route is point-only: exact laws are evaluated and counted laws are `cell_not_licensed`.

## ADMG conditional transport

`identify_admg_conditional_transport` (Python) and `decide_admg_conditional_transport` (Rust) answer the target's `P*(y | do(x), w)` over a selection ADMG with at most {{bound:transport.admg_conditional.max_observed}} observed variables, 0-{{bound:transport.admg_conditional.max_treatments}} treatments and 1-{{bound:transport.admg_conditional.max_conditioned}} conditioned variables, from one source that can run every experiment plus the target's observational law.

- **Reduction.** Rule 2 of the do-calculus, applied in the target, moves a conditioned variable `w` into the intervention set when the outcome is m-separated from `w` given the treatments and the other conditioned variables in the appropriately mutilated graph. The classical sID engine then decides the reduced joint, and the answer is that joint normalized at the requested levels of the remaining conditioned variables. A zero-mass conditioning event is `transport_support_failure` (`admg_transport.support_failure`).
- **Guarantee.** Sound and incomplete. Every decision re-checks its own reduction with an independent separation test before returning, and a stored derivation re-runs the checks when prepared and when an artifact is consumed.
- **Obstruction.** When sID does not certify the reduced joint, a bounded search looks for a two-model witness: two finite latent models compatible with the selection diagram that agree on every source experimental law and on the target observational law yet give different values of the query. An exactly verified pair is `proven_non_transportable` (`admg_transport.proven_non_transportable`) with a `ConditionalNonTransportabilityProof`; with no verified pair, or above the work bound, the decision stays `not_certified` (`admg_transport.not_certified`) with an inspection-only candidate. A proven obstruction exports separately and its consumer re-verifies the witness exactly.
- **Budget.** One `SearchBudget` (at most {{bound:transport.admg_conditional.max_search_states}} operations, depth {{bound:transport.admg_conditional.max_depth}}, and a memory cap that is never absent) covers every separation test, sID step, catalog binding and witness search. A stop is `exhausted` with a receipt, never a verdict.
- **Not licensed.** Counted laws, an empirical plug-in and any interval (`cell_not_licensed`, `admg_transport.interval_withheld`); selection-bias (`S = 1` sampling) semantics; soft interventions; surrogate or heterogeneous experiments. Use the [mixed-source proof route](#mixed-source-proof-search) for a declared evidence catalog.

## Smoothed dose-response transport

One cell: a randomized continuous source dose with a **known** conditional density `pi(a | x)` on a declared support, transported to an overlap-supported target, on a grid of at most {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_grid_points}} doses at one declared bandwidth `h` with the Epanechnikov kernel. The estimand is `psi_h(a) = E_target[ integral K_h(a - t) E(Y | X, A=t, S=1) dt ]`; the bandwidth and kernel are part of the estimand and are never tuned. The derivation of the score and its model double robustness are in [smoothed dose-response transport](../architecture/smoothed-dose-response-transport.md). The refusal namespace is `dose_response`.

- **Surface.** Python `advanced.prepare_smoothed_dose`, `advanced.consume_smoothed_dose`, `advanced.smoothed_dose_estimator_menu`; Rust `StudyBuilder::smoothed_dose_transport`.
- **Support.** Every window `[a - h, a + h]` must lie in the dose support (`dose_response.grid_outside_dose_support`); known densities must be valid and above a floor; the local kernel-weight effective sample size and distinct doses per window are gated; membership overlap is gated as in the learned-trial cell. An estimated density, point-curve, stochastic, derivative, conditional or simultaneous target is refused.
- **Nuisances.** The dose-by-covariate outcome regression and membership are `LearnerSpec` learners cross-fitted on one shared fold assignment; every fold model is kept as a portable predictor, so a learner without one is refused (`dose_response.learner_not_portable`).
- **Errors kept apart.** Quadrature error (estimated per grid dose, refused above the declared tolerance, `dose_response.quadrature_tolerance`) and the smoothing-bias diagnostic `psi_h - psi_{h/2}` are reported and neither is added to the estimate. The analytic influence-function standard error is a diagnostic only.
- **Bounds.** At most {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_grid_points}} grid doses, {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_folds}} cross-fitting folds, {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_rows}} rows, {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_features}} covariates, basis degree 1 to {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_basis_degree}}, at most {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_knots}} knots, and a bootstrap request of {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.min_bootstrap}} to {{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_bootstrap}} replicates (or none). A request above a cap refuses as `dose_response.bounds_exceeded`; 1 to 198 replicates is below the floor, so the point is kept and the interval withheld (`dose_response.bootstrap_below_floor`). A workspace cap of 512 MiB ({{bound:transport.smoothed_dose_response.max_workspace_bytes}} bytes) refuses before any fit.
- **Interval.** The pointwise outer-refit percentile bootstrap route is closed (`cell_not_licensed`).

## Exact binary observation recovery

Graph-licensed recovery, not MAR or IPCW. A separate recovery stage recovers the full law `P(X(1), O)` of item-missing binary variables from one named observed pattern law `P(R, X*, O)` (`identify_observation_recovery`), then feeds it to the ordinary target identification stage for a causal effect.

- **Class.** A binary DAG m-graph: at most {{bound:recovery.binary_observation.max_partially_observed}} partially observed variables `X`, each with a response indicator `R` (parents among `X` and `O` only) and a deterministic proxy `X*` with levels `0`, `1`, `?`; at most {{bound:recovery.binary_observation.max_fully_observed}} fully observed `O`; at most {{bound:recovery.binary_observation.max_observed_cells}} observed cells. Selection nodes, bidirected edges, non-binary variables, `R -> R` edges and noisy proxies refuse as `recovery.unsupported_mechanism`.
- **Decision.** Recoverable exactly when no `X_i -> R_i` edge exists. A self-censoring edge is refused as not recoverable for every model Markov to the m-graph, shown by an exact-integer witness (`recovery.nonrecoverable_witness`, `transport_proven_non_transportable`). The witness models are degenerate, so nothing is claimed under faithful or generic parameters, where some self-censoring graphs are generically identified by an assumption this route does not make.
- **Evidence.** One joint law over exactly `R`, `X*`, `O`; separate marginals refuse as `recovery.missing_margin`. No complete-case fallback and no MAR or IPCW substitution.
- **Recovered law.** A derived law carrying recovered provenance. It never supplies a population law and satisfies no catalog-route factor; the mixed-source search excludes it. The effect handoff requires the same population, the variables `X ∪ O`, the m-graph's causal restriction as the effect graph (`recovery.handoff_mismatch`) and strictly positive support.
- **Budget.** One `SearchBudget` (at most {{bound:recovery.binary_observation.max_search_operations}} operations, depth {{bound:recovery.binary_observation.max_depth}}); a stop is `recovery.budget` with a receipt, never a verdict.
- **Claim.** Exact laws only and point-only; counted laws refuse as `cell_not_licensed`. A bootstrap interval for sampled recovery is described in [interval for sampled recovery](../sampled-recovery-bca.md).

## Study planning over multi-source and mixed-source catalogs

Given a query the multi-source or mixed-source route cannot identify from its catalog, and a declared universe of candidate studies, `plan_studies` (Python `plan_studies`) finds the cheapest subset that would make the same route identify the query if the studies delivered exactly the declared regimes. This is structural planning with declared costs, not a success probability.

- **Candidates.** A study is a population (the target or a declared source), an intervention set with its feasible level combinations, the jointly measured margin, a recruitment declaration, a positive integer cost, a sample budget (a tie-breaker only) and `requires` and `conflicts` constraints. Anything the route cannot accept refuses as `study_plan.invalid_candidate`.
- **Search.** At most {{bound:design.study_planning.max_candidates}} candidates, {{bound:design.study_planning.max_regimes_per_candidate}} regimes each; every subset of at most {{bound:design.study_planning.max_subset_size}} is evaluated in cost order by re-running the route's decision on a preview catalog. One `SearchBudget` (at most {{bound:design.study_planning.max_search_states}} operations, depth {{bound:design.study_planning.max_depth}}, {{bound:design.study_planning.max_depth_mixed}} on the mixed route, and 512 MiB; at most {{bound:design.study_planning.max_proposals}} proposals retained) bounds the work; a stop keeps the proposals verified so far with a receipt (`study_plan.budget`).
- **Sufficiency and minimality.** A proposal is sufficient only when the re-run decision identifies, its derivation re-checks, and its formula cites a proposed regime. No sufficient subset is `study_plan.none_certified` within the universe, never an impossibility claim. The top proposal is cost-minimal only among the verified subsets of at most {{bound:design.study_planning.max_subset_size}} candidates of the declared universe, under this search.
- **Arrival.** `receive` requires the frozen base unchanged and each proposed regime present as available evidence of exactly the proposed shape, bound to the provider's snapshot (`study_plan.arrival_mismatch`), then decides the real catalog through the public route. Positivity is a premise: a law without mass at a level the formula reads passes `receive` and is refused by the ordinary evaluator.

## Path-specific edge intervention

One cell on the two-world cross-world contrast: how much of the treatment effect flows along a chosen set of edges of an explicit Markovian DAG while every other edge sees the baseline value. The graph has at most {{bound:counterfactual.path_specific_edge.max_variables}} variables, a query declares at most {{bound:counterfactual.path_specific_edge.max_worlds}} worlds, and a consumer admits at most {{bound:counterfactual.path_specific_edge.max_rows}} rows; the claim is a point, never an interval. Out-of-contract shapes refuse with `cross_world.*` details (for example `cross_world.graph_outside_contract`). Latent confounding is handled by the next route.

## ADMG counterfactual identification

One cell: the effect of treatment on the treated, `P(Y_x = y | X = x')` with `x != x'`, on a supplied explicit ADMG (or DAG) of at most {{bound:counterfactual.admg_identification.max_variables}} finite-discrete variables with at most {{bound:counterfactual.admg_identification.max_levels}} levels each, from the observational joint (exact law or empirical counts). Each district term is identified by ID under one `SearchBudget` (at most {{bound:counterfactual.admg_identification.max_search_operations}} operations, depth {{bound:counterfactual.admg_identification.max_search_depth}}); Python `antecedent.counterfactual_id.prepare_effect_on_treated`. When that stops and the treatment is binary, the consistency identity `P(Y_x = y, X = x') = P_x(y) - P(y, X = x)` answers wherever ID identifies `P_x(y)`.

A query this route does not identify refuses with `route_not_supported` and a checkable obstruction. That is not a proof of non-identifiability: the composition of ID* with ID is not complete from `P(V)`. The claim is a point and never carries an interval. Path-specific queries are outside this route; the [transported path-specific adapter](../transport-counterfactuals.md) supports only its declared affine-additive model.

## General finite counterfactual transport

One contract, separate from the effect-on-the-treated route and from the interventional transport routes: a nested or conditional counterfactual probability `P*(event | conditioning)` of a target population, from population- and regime-labelled exact finite laws of the target and of source populations whose mechanisms may differ at declared variables. The theorem family is Correa, Lee and Bareinboim (ICML 2022); the implementation is a sound, bounded decision procedure of that shape and completeness is not claimed.

The query is a `CounterfactualEventQuery`: worlds with hard interventions and edge routes (so `Y_{x, M_{x'}}`, natural direct and indirect effects and the effect of treatment on the treated share one form), an event conjunction and an optional conditioning conjunction. Bounds: at most 8 observed variables with 4 declared levels each, 6 worlds, 16 atoms, 24 network nodes, 4 sources with at most 4 controllable variables each, and 64 catalog regimes; larger requests are `route_not_supported` (`counterfactual_transport.bounds_exceeded`), never truncated. The task page is [Transport counterfactual and sampled quantities](../transport-counterfactuals.md), which also covers the Python surface.

The outcomes are kept apart. *Identified* carries the derivation, the cited regimes and a replayable receipt. *Missing evidence* names the declared regimes whose laws would license a derivation. A *proven obstruction* is only ever a two-model witness re-verified by exact rational enumeration. *Not certified* means the bounded search found neither, and a query that is non-transportable only through a witness outside the searched family stays here. A budget or cancellation stop is a receipt with no verdict.

Evaluation reads exact laws. A factor undefined because a law gives its conditioning event zero mass, or because no law was supplied at a needed intervention level, is never given a value (`transport_support_failure`, `transport_missing_provider`). Counted, empirical or posterior laws are refused here (`cell_not_licensed`); sampled evidence uses the route described in the task page.

## Estimation assumptions

`EmpiricalTable` is the conservative default for count-based routes. `LearnedCategorical` and `TrialAipw` change the assumption set and must be passed explicitly. Exact laws make no sampling-coverage claim.

Percentile-bootstrap and analytic-standard-error intervals exist for specific classical constructions, and the measured joint Bayesian, nested-Markov, dependent temporal and sampled-recovery intervals are described in [Population, time and uncertainty routes](../population-time-uncertainty.md). Whether any of them carries a coverage record in your build is on `result.inspect().calibration`. Multi-source calibrated coverage and simultaneous grid bands remain closed.
