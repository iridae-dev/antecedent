# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added

- **Discovery validation through the compiler.** `Study::validate_discovery` (Python `antecedent.discovery_validation`) runs the block-bootstrap, false-positive, tuning, synthetic-null, environment hold-out and regime-stability checks of a compiled temporal structure search on the study's own data, each as a compiled program with a contract and typed refusals. Selective discovery and partially observed signed-uniform marginals compile the same way.
- **Sensitivity ranges on a prepared study's own compiled result.** `PreparedStudy::sensitivity_range` and `sensitivity_vector_range` (Python `antecedent.validation.sensitivity_range` and `sensitivity_vector_range`) compute the E-value, omitted-variable-bias, mediation, marginal sensitivity model, posterior and marginal structural model parameter ranges from what the study identified, bound to its target. A longitudinal regime study retains the confounder columns it declares (`LongitudinalRegime(covariates=...)`) so the marginal structural model refit can read them.
- **Fitted-model operations on a prepared study.** `PreparedStudy::model_operation` runs edge strengths, the population do-contrast, the do-sampler, model evaluation and nested counterfactuals on the study's own fitted model; `PreparedStudy::falsify_graph` runs the permutation falsification of its declared DAG. A process-local feedback system of declared callback mechanisms (`compile_local_cyclic`) and the transported static path-specific counterfactual (`TransportedCounterfactual::compile`) also compile.
- **Nested counterfactuals and the population do-contrast as queries.** `SimulationTarget::NestedCounterfactual` and `AttributeTarget::PopulationDoContrast` pose them as `Simulate` and `Attribute` queries with a query-level contract and claim (Python `antecedent.model_operations.NestedCounterfactualMean`; `PopulationContrast` now runs the `Attribute` query). New wire variants `SimulationTargetWire::NestedCounterfactual` and `AttributeTargetWire::PopulationDoContrast` (and the `SimulateQueryWire::all_interventions` reader); existing wire bytes, labels and identities are unchanged. The `ModelOperation` forms of both are kept, run through the same executor and give the same numbers.
- **Compiled decisions and planning over supplied evidence.** Decisions over supplied claims, identified sets of utility, decision contracts, inverse queries and decision draws, and the planning searches (identification repair, study addition, design and choice-study ranking, evidence obligations, adaptive policy search), compile into programs with canonical identities.
- **Compiled transport identification and descriptive estimates.** Classical, z-, mz-, mixed-source, ADMG-conditional and counterfactual transport identification, observation and chain recovery, the descriptive comparison of two arms, the matched case-control odds ratio and the lagged linear conditional prediction each compile through their own program family.
- **Compiled priors, recovery and measurement corrections.** External prior composition and transport, power-prior discount selection, single-source z-transport estimates, observation and chain recovery, exposure misclassification, soft-exposure and detection corrections, repeat codings, measured-exposure imputation and pooling, nested Markov intervals and posteriors, learned and Bayesian joint transport, and the sample-split measured effects each run as a compiled program family.
- **Compiled randomized, policy and transport routes.** Factorial and net IPW policy kernels (with `BinaryPolicyConstraints` for capacity and budget limits), same-sample (estimated-slope) CUPED declared on the study with `StudyBuilder::randomized_adjustment(RandomizedAdjustment::EstimatedCuped { covariate })` (estimator `randomized.estimated_cuped_ht_itt`, a new `EstimatorId::RandomizedEstimatedCupedHt` variant; Python `RandomizedEffect(estimated_cuped=EstimatedCUPED(...))`), supplied transport outcome regressions, the augmented transport response grid, scenario sets, measurement-model and pooled differential transport, and transported cohort effects compile through Study. Caller-supplied learner factories are declared on the study (`StudyBuilder::learner_factories`), and a study that declares none keeps its identity. The same declaration (`LearnerFactories`) fits the trial-to-target AIPW transport (`Study::compile_trial_aipw_learners`) and the factorized joint cells (`Study::compile_joint_cells_learners`) through the compiler, point-only and labelled with the declared learners so library-learner coverage never annotates them; the direct `estimate_trial_aipw_with_factories` and `fit_factorized_joint_cells_with_factories` entries are deprecated in their favour. New `PlanRoute` / `ProgramCell` variants: `TrialAipwLearnerTransport`, `JointCellsLearnerFit`.
- **Compiled measured intervals and fits.** The adaptive and choice result intervals, the cohort mean interval, the count-fitted interval and calibrated probability, the temporal dependent interval, the bias-aware dose grid, caller-learner smoothed dose transport and external feedback fits each run as a compiled program; every interference design runs through analyze with its own coverage key.
- **`WeightedTarget::unit_effects` and `WeightedTarget::min_ess_fraction`** read back the effects and the ESS-fraction floor a weighted transport target was declared with.
- **Marked Hawkes kernels, excitation challenges and the parameter-intervention bridge.** Each runs as a compiled program under its own support unit. `Study::prepare_marked_likelihood`, `prepare_marked_fit` and `prepare_marked_draw` read a finite-rank kernel on declared mark prototypes (`forecast.marked_event_process`). `Study::prepare_excitation_offspring`, `prepare_marked_offspring`, `prepare_excitation_challenge`, `prepare_marked_challenge`, `prepare_release_comparison` and `prepare_marked_release_comparison` report horizon and lifetime offspring, challenge the direct mass against a declared immigrant trend, and compare release times under an agreed reading (`forecast.hawkes_excitation`). `Study::prepare_excitation_question`, `prepare_release_question`, `prepare_release_law`, `prepare_intervened_law`, `prepare_shock_query` and `prepare_release_study` carry an agreed direct mass (or, under an identity map, the lifetime total offspring of a mutually exciting pair) through the parameter-intervention bridge read against every member of a declared structure, draw one release action as one weighted mixture law over members that orient the edge and members that do not, hand an excluded shock to a lag-count query, and price observing the stream for the release; `prepare_member_release_study` prices it per member with the range and, for weighted structures, the mixture (`forecast.hawkes_parameter_bridge`). The structure is a `DeclaredStructure` of the new shared member carrier `antecedent::analysis::structure_members`: one graph, an equivalence class read through its completions, an explicit candidate set, a DAG or ADMG graph posterior, or a cyclic system, each member identified as `identify_structure` identifies it; `AcceptedExcitation::structure` reports the per-member readings, their envelope, the identified weight and the mixture conditional on identified members. A `&AcceptedGraph` still converts, and a single accepted DAG keeps its result and program identity. Python reaches all of them through `antecedent.events.prepare`. New `PlanRoute` / `ProgramCell` variants: `MarkedEventProcess`, `HawkesExcitation`, `HawkesParameterBridge`. New `EventLawMeaning::Intervened` variant for an intervened continuation. New `EventError::BridgeRefused` variant (appended last): each refusal of the parameter-intervention bridge and of the structural reading it cites carries its own registered `events.*` detail and reason code. `PreparedEventStudy::release_information` says whether a study was compiled on a kernel a bridge kept. The dense event catalog cap is now derived from the operation budget (`MAX_EVENT_TYPES`), and event worlds accept up to 63 intervention worlds.
- **Every artifact loader admits an artifact as the one its consumer retained.** Each loader in `antecedent-io-core`, `antecedent-io`, `antecedent-design` and `antecedent::io` gains an `*_expecting` form (`*_retained` where its name already ends in `_expecting` or `_expected`) that takes a `RetainedArtifactIdentity` the consumer kept apart from the bytes (`RetainedArtifactIdentity::of_bytes` when the artifact is written) and binds the bytes to it through `bound_identity::bind` before reading them; other bytes are refused with `external_binding_mismatch`. Python loaders take `retained_identity=` (`antecedent.artifacts.artifact_identity(data)` computes it) and go through the same native check. The unbound forms are unchanged; Python warns with `AntecedentDeprecationWarning` when a loader admits an artifact with no retained or expected identity.
- **Decisions across trade-offs.** A decision can now be made when the weights between outcomes are uncertain. A value model over named outcome attributes ranges its trade-off parameters over a declared set (`antecedent_design::preference_model::ValueModel`, `PreferenceSet`; `PreferenceContract` reuses the decision contract's criteria, constraints and structural policies, and a value that names no trade-off is the ordinary contract with the same identity, result and replay bytes). The exact engine answers an affine value with an expectation criterion by checked linear programs; the certified engine answers any other value or criterion by outward-rounded subdivision and reports the undecided share. Each result says which actions are best at every admissible trade-off, which can be best, each action's value range and worst-case regret, and where the choice switches; a range is a declared assumption and is never called a probability. Across structures, unidentified and unevaluated mass is reported and never renormalized, and the members of a CPDAG or PAG atom are weighed by a declared `ClassPrior` (`uniform`, `from_pairs`, `exact_limit`, `sample`), enumerated up to an exact limit and sampled uniformly above it with Clopper-Pearson mass bounds. In Python, `decision.Contract` takes `value=decision.Value(...)` and `policy=`; `utility_units`, `criterion` and `target_population` default to `None` and stay required unless `value=` is given, and existing calls construct and evaluate as before. Every Python decision result carries `standing` and `statement`.
- **Compiled decision and learning programs.** `Contract.compile` lowers a decision into the program of the analysis that estimates its quantities (`Study::compile_for_preference_decision`, `compile_for_contract_decision`, `compile_preference_decision_across_studies`, `compile_preference_decision_with_shared_evidence`, `PreparedNativeJointDesign::compile_for_preference_decision`). A source already in hand is decided by the programs `TradeoffLawDecision`, `TradeoffCertifiedLawDecision`, `TradeoffStructuralDecision` and `TradeoffCertifiedStructuralDecision`, and `antecedent.decision_learning` learns trade-offs from recorded choices through `PreferenceCompatibleSet` (the compatible set) and `PreferenceChoiceModel` (a conditional logit with delta-method intervals, calibration `not_measured` until a coverage record exists); reviewed, versioned `AcceptedPreferences` are read by a contract as its value. New `ProgramCell` variants: `TradeoffLawDecision`, `TradeoffCertifiedLawDecision`, `TradeoffStructuralDecision`, `TradeoffCertifiedStructuralDecision`, `PreferenceCompatibleSet`, `PreferenceChoiceModel`, and for the parent programs `PreferenceAttachedDecision`, `CertifiedPreferenceAttachedDecision`, `ContractAttachedDecision`, `JointScenarioAttachedDecision`, `EvsiAttachedDecision`. Studies that share observations are found by row and unit provenance (`StudyBuilder::unit_key`, `StudyBuilder::row_provenance`) and need a declared cross-study covariance. Supporting kernels: `antecedent_stats::{linear_program, glm::conditional_logit, glm::nonlinear_conditional_logit, binomial_interval}`, uniform Markov-equivalence-class and PAG member sampling in `antecedent_graph`, and the independent-unit bootstrap of cluster-randomized, matched-pair and switchback designs (`unit_bootstrap::joint_unit_bootstrap`, read through `NativeJointDesignResult::unit_bootstrap`).
- **Decisions attached to an analysis are programs of their own.** A decision compiled with the studies, the compiled design or the chain that estimate its quantities (`Study::compile_for_preference_decision`, `compile_for_contract_decision`, `compile_*_decision_across_studies`, the design entries) and the robust decision over joint scenarios (`compile_for_robust_decision`) compile to a parent program composed over those child programs. Its identity is the children's program identities in declared order and the decision's declaration; `CompiledPreferenceDecision::plan_view` and `CompiledRobustDecision::decision_plan_view` report the decision's own unit (`preference_decision.exact` or `.certified`, `decision.contract`, `decision.compiled_joint_scenarios`) while each child keeps its own. An execution passes the decision's gate before any child runs: a release build refuses a decision whose unit is not supported, and a development build records it (`PreferenceDecisionExecution::would_refuse`). The receipt names the decision's program (`PreferenceDecisionReceipt::decision_program`). `Study::compile_decision_program` and `Study::compile_evsi_program` do the same for a terminal decision (`decision.contract`) and a value-of-information request (`design.evsi`): they compile the same plan as their child and run the same scorer or EVSI engine after the gate; `design_ranking::evaluate_for_study` and the Python decision compile run through them. No decision result changes.
- **Vocabulary for decisions across trade-offs.** New wire variants `ClassPriorIdentityWire::Uniform` and `ClassPriorIdentityWire::Annotated` (a declared assumption, exact limit or sample beside the masses), `StructuralWeightBasisWire::PosteriorWithDeclaredClassPrior` and `StructuralWeightBasisWire::SampledClassMeasure` (and the same on `StructuralWeightBasis`), and the query variant `JointDesignInference::IndependentUnitBootstrap` (under a declared sampling premise). Existing wire bytes, labels and identities are unchanged.
- **Draft propagation chains.** A chain joining separately fitted studies compiles before anything is fitted, with its family table deferred (`PropagationChainPlan::is_deferred`, `execute_bound`, `deferred_family_table`). The draft travels in the new `antecedent_io_core::PropagationChainDraftWire` (`record`, `deferred`) beside the record, carries the required feature `propagation_chain_deferred_sources_v1`, and is never sealed, exported or consumed until its table is bound (`propagation_chain.deferred_source_unbound`). `PropagationChainRecordWire` and the bytes and identity of every released record are unchanged.

### Deprecated

- Facade artifact loaders that admit an artifact without an identity its consumer retained (`antecedent::io::{load_analysis_result, replay_analysis_result, decode_analysis_result_artifact, decode_causal_posterior_bytes, decode_causal_payload_artifact, decode_prior_source_meta, decode_model_bundle_bytes}`, `io::policy_program_artifact::{decode_policy_program, decode_policy_program_with}`, `io::provider_envelope::open_provider_envelope`, `io::joint_panel_artifact::consume_panel_execution`, `io::mnar_candidate_source::decode_source`, `io::release_migration::{consume_previous_release_artifact, consume_previous_route_records}`, `analysis::design_ranking::consume` and `design::transported_decision_artifact::consume_transported_decision`) are `#[deprecated(since = "3.1.0")]` wrappers in favour of their `*_expecting` forms with a `RetainedArtifactIdentity`; the Python loaders warn when called with neither `retained_identity=` nor an expected identity. The kernel crates are not deprecated. The unbound forms are removed in 4.0.
- Sensitivity functions that take supplied estimates or draws, the direct target-native checks, the direct fitted-model (`FittedGcm`) operations, and the other facade and Python paths that reach these computations without the compiler are deprecated in favour of the compiled replacements above. A replacement calls the same kernel and returns the same numbers; each deprecation note states any difference in the path. The direct paths are removed in 4.0.
- The facade's re-exports of kernel run entries are now `#[deprecated(since = "3.1.0")]` wrappers with the kernel signature, so a caller sees the warning and the compiled replacement it names; the kernel crates themselves are not deprecated. This includes `discover_and_transport` and `discover_per_population` (compiled `DiscoveredClassTransport` and `PerPopulationStructureSearch`; Python `PerPopulationDiscovery.run` in favour of `compiled_run`) and `estimate_trial_aipw_with_factories` and `fit_factorized_joint_cells_with_factories` (compiled `TrialAipwLearnerTransport` and `JointCellsLearnerFit`).
- `Study::compile_for_decision` and `Study::compile_for_evsi`, in favour of `Study::compile_decision_program` and `Study::compile_evsi_program`, which return the same plan (`plan()`, `into_plan()`) with the decision's own program gated on its unit; signatures are unchanged.

### Fixed

- **Exact-law content identity v2.** `ExactDiscreteLaw::content_identity` now writes every field on the canonical writer (identity scheme v2); the v1 scheme built a `Debug` descriptor joined with `:`, so a population or snapshot text containing the separator could give two different laws one identity, and the order the intervention world was listed in changed it. The v1 identity stays available as `ExactDiscreteLaw::content_identity_v1`, `ExactDiscreteLaw::accepts_content_identity` accepts either, and a counterfactual transport artifact sealed under v1 still replays. Identities that fold in a law identity (compiled transport scenario and z-transport programs, recalculation stage identities, cited laws of new counterfactual transport artifacts) change once.
- **Static graph identities carry node labels.** The static DAG and ADMG identity wires name nodes by dense index, so the same edges over different variables, or one graph built in another dense order, could share an identification identity. `dag_identity` and `admg_identity` now write a graph whose dense nodes are `Static(0..n)` in any order in the unlabeled form (a graph built in the aligned order keeps its identity) and any other graph in the new wire variants `GraphIdentityWire::LabeledDag` and `GraphIdentityWire::LabeledAdmg` (`LabeledStaticGraphIdentityWire`). Stored identification wires keep verifying: the digest is over the stored wire.
- **Structure-prior edge order.** `StructurePriorSpec`'s canonical encoding sorts per-edge entries by the whole entry (`from`, `to`, probability bits) instead of `(from, to)` alone, so an unvalidated map that lists one edge twice encodes alike in any order. Validation refuses a repeated edge, so no valid prior's identity changes.
- **Static CPDAG and PAG identities carry node labels; the transport selection graph is in variable space.** `cpdag_identity` and `pag_identity` write a schema-aligned graph (nodes `Static(0..n)` in any dense order) in the unlabeled form, so a graph built in the aligned order keeps its identity, and any other graph in the new wire variants `GraphIdentityWire::LabeledCpdag` and `GraphIdentityWire::LabeledPag` (`LabeledMarkedGraphIdentityWire`). The selection graph of `TransportIdentityWire` is written with each variable at its own index, so a selection diagram over other variables, or built in another dense order, no longer shares transport premises; a selection graph with a non-static node is refused. Graph-posterior atoms go through the labelled identities and refuse a non-aligned atom graph (atoms are built over the schema variables, so stored atoms are unchanged). Stored identification wires keep verifying: the digest is over the stored wire.
- **Propagation-chain factor kinds without a canonical tag are refused.** A shared-factor kind added later without a tag would have shared tag 0 with any other; the chain identity now refuses it. Every current kind keeps its tag.
- **Temporal counterfactual request identity v2.** The fixed-population temporal counterfactual `spec_id` now writes every list (edges, coefficients, world units) with its length (tag `temporal_counterfactual_v2.spec`); the v1 stream had no list lengths, so where one list ended was not in the bytes. Artifacts sealed under v1 still replay, and a retained v1 identity still matches.
- **Temporal recalculation premises digest v2.** Bidirected pairs are unordered and selection targets a set in `TemporalRecalcArtifactWire::expected_premises_digest`; a stored v1 digest still validates (`premises_digest_v1`, `premises_digest_verifies`).
- **Framed regime ids of redrawn draws.** A variable or level containing `=`, `,`, `(` or `)` no longer imitates two assignments in the regime id of an observation query's parameter draws (it is written as `do[...]` of quoted pairs); other names keep their id, and a stored v1 alignment identity still replays.
- **Set-valued declarations written once.** Repeated exact entries are written once in observation-query assignment lists, transported counterfactual selection nodes, covariate-shift modifiers, cross-world graph edges and composition shared-data relations (which are also ordered by the whole relation); stored artifacts in the earlier form still verify. A sensitivity grid holding both `-0.0` and `0.0` is refused as one point listed twice.
- **Mechanism-composition plan identity binds sets as sets.** A plan's parents, conditioning variables, latent causes and their peers, and transport admissions are deduplicated, and admissions and latents are ordered by every field, so a repeated declaration or two admissions differing only in their basis no longer make `MechanismCompositionPlan::identity_hex` depend on listing order. Plans without repeats keep their identities.
- **Transported-claim offer and handoff identities read the transport as sets.** `TransportedClaimOffer::canonical_identity` and the transported handoff identity digest the transport's populations and assumptions deduplicated (`TransportIdentity::set_digest`, `TransportIdentity::as_sets`), as the ledger and the handoff wire already did, so a handoff sealed from an offer that repeated a population now verifies on consume. Offers without repeats keep their identities.
- **Choice-contract identity version 2.** A retained source's observation ids enter `ChoiceDesignContract::identity` as a set of rows (`a` and `a#0` are one row; order and repetition do not count), as the evidence ledger accounts them. `CHOICE_DESIGN_VERSION` is 2; `ChoiceDesignContract::accepts_identity_hex` accepts version-1 identities, and a choice-inference result artifact sealed under version 1 still replays.
- **Checked world-point proof identity treats each parent's evidence as a set.** The order and repetition of a parent claim's evidence references no longer change `CheckedWorldPoint::proof_identity`.
- **Wider frame fingerprints.** `FrameLedger` and `FrameContents` fingerprints sum 512-bit row digests instead of 256-bit ones, so crafted row sets that collide under an additive multiset hash are out of reach. Fingerprints are compared in memory and never stored.
- **Study candidate semantic identity v2.** `DurableStudyCandidate::semantic_id` reads variable lists, feasibility notes, levels and expected laws as sets. A candidate whose free text or levels contain a v1 separator (`;`, `|`, `[`, `]`), or that has an empty note, cluster or provider, now gets a framed `sc2:` identity, because its v1 content string could collide (notes `["a|b"]` and `["a", "b"]` shared one). Any other candidate keeps its `sc1:` identity. `DurableStudyCandidate::semantic_id_v1` and `accepts_semantic_id` verify stored v1 identities; repair artifacts, proposal receipts and proposal arrival accept either.
- **Evidence plan identity v2.** `EvidencePlan::identity` now length-prefixes an option's resolved and unresolved obligation ids and each unverified route's obligation ids. Before, moving an id from one list to the other left the identity unchanged. `EvidencePlan::identity_v1` and `accepts_identity` verify stored v1 identities, and evidence plan artifacts accept either.
- **Decision constraints read `applies_to` as a set.** A repeated action id in a hard constraint's `applies_to` no longer changes the decision contract identity. Contracts without such a repeat keep their identity, and `DecisionContract::accepts_identity` and `AdmissibleDecisionContract::accepts_identity` verify identities stored before the change, which robust decision artifacts and compiled scenario results replay under.
- **Composition bundles frame provider and data references.** A reference whose provider, snapshot, request or digest field contains `|` is digested with framed fields, because two such references could share a node digest. Bundles sealed before the change still verify.
- **External-claim evidence digest.** The composition verifier's evidence digest of an external claim treats each id list as a set and frames every id, so a comma inside an id no longer splits it.
- **Joint experiment compilation refuses a repeated adjustment.** `compile_joint_experiment` refuses two adjustments for one outcome, as `JointExperimentEvidence::new` does. Before, their order reached the adjustment identity.
- **Sequential model identity treats boundary states as a set.** `FiniteSequentialModel::identity_hex` writes each stopping boundary's monitored states in ascending order, so listing them in another order no longer changes the model identity. The earlier form stays available as `FiniteSequentialModel::identity_hex_v1`, and `SequentialEvaluationArtifact::verify` accepts a stored model identity under either. Models whose boundaries list their states ascending keep their identity.
- **Merged transported points bind their members' values.** `TransportedResponse::merge_points` derives the merged source identity from each member's identity, coordinates and means (tag `antecedent.transported_response.merge.v2`); the earlier identity bound only the member identities, so two merges of the same members with different values shared it. Merged points get a new source identity once; stored receipts are validated as stored.
- **Robust-decision wires write sets once and in order.** The result and claim-profile wires of `RobustResultArtifact` write tied leaders, graph-dependent leaders and unsupported structure ids sorted and deduplicated, and order shortfalls and support rows by the whole entry. Stored artifacts still load and replay: replay compares both results through the same canonical wire.
- **Stored z-transport snapshots need a schema-aligned selection diagram.** A diagram whose node `i` is not variable `i` is refused by the snapshot digest instead of being stored under a dense-index wire that names other variables.
- **Decision-draw digests break a repeated candidate id by content.** Only an unvalidated carrier can repeat an id; its digest no longer depends on listing order.
- **Hand-built dynamic schedules are validated.** `TemporalPolicy::validate` refuses a `TemporalPolicy::Dynamic` whose `active_at` is not strictly ascending with the new `InterventionError::UnorderedDynamicSchedule`; activity is looked up by binary search and the identity writes the offsets as given, so an unsorted or repeated schedule answered wrongly and spelled one schedule twice. `TemporalPolicy::dynamic` already built the canonical form.
- A fresh-process recalculation plan no longer reports frozen portable scores as reused when the data snapshot was not declared when they were frozen and its data were not supplied: the score artifact is refused for missing data, as it already was for a changed snapshot.
- **Sealed off-axis compiled results verify.** A compiled `Simulate`, `Attribute`, `TemporalDesign`, `IvBounds` or `Association` result whose claim carries a calibration basis names its own query family in that basis; the contract verifier accepted an off-axis basis only under `Unknown` or `Discover`, so `encode_contracted_result` refused a `GcmSimulateDag` result with `claim.calibration.basis`. An off-axis basis may now name the query family of the contract's own target.

## 3.0.0

Changes since 2.4.1. The [migration guide](docs/migration.md) says who needs to act and gives the old and new form of every breaking change and what to regenerate; the [release notes](docs/release-notes/v3.0.0.md) describe the new features.

### Removed

- Removed six deprecated Rust limit and refusal-detail constants and the `refuse_pag_transport` constructor. The [Rust migration guide](docs/migration.md#retired-rust-limits-and-pag-refusal-constructor) lists every symbol and replacement.

### Added

- **Feedback and dynamic systems.** Cyclic routes (`antecedent.cyclic`, `Study::prepare_cyclic`) answer what happens when a component of a system with feedback is observed, clamped or shocked, as a model-conditional response with declared equilibrium and selection assumptions. See [cyclic systems](docs/guides/cyclic-systems.md).
- **External cyclic mechanisms.** `antecedent.external_mechanisms.register_mechanism` (Rust: `ExternalMechanismSpec`) binds a mechanism the library does not own to a versioned implementation id, content digest, dependencies, signature, parameter schema and derivative declaration. A fitted feedback system that uses it records those identities, never the code, and replays in another process only through a caller-supplied trusted resolver whose implementation is verified against the record. Such a system is `verified_external`, never `native`; the library does not sandbox the callable. See [registered external cyclic mechanisms](docs/external-cyclic-mechanisms.md).
- **Joint intervention programs.** `InterventionContrast::linear` and the joint-response routes evaluate a coordinated program over a horizon against a baseline with one joint uncertainty from the shared fit, and search an explicitly enumerated program domain. See [joint intervention responses](docs/guides/joint-intervention-responses.md).
- **Joint panel models.** `antecedent.joint_panel` fits a multichannel panel once (distributed lags, saturation, group and seasonal effects, an observed confounder and AR(1) dependence within groups) and retains shared draws for every world. `family=` selects geometric adstock, Hill shapes, class-pooled half-saturation or spatially correlated group intercepts; categorical channels are dummy-coded, missing outcomes and confounders can be multiply imputed, and a declared Gaussian restriction is retained as its own identification attempt. A fit of any family identifies no world; the declared design does. See [fit and compare coordinated intervention programs](docs/guides/joint-intervention-responses.md) and [update a joint response with experiments and designs](docs/guides/joint-program-designs.md).
- **Evidence to decisions to the next study.** A retained response law carries into experimental evidence through an explicit estimand mapping, updates a decision bound to its evidence, and ranks further studies by information value and cost. Withdrawing or superseding a source invalidates current decision use.
- **Decision evidence from several experimental outcomes.** `antecedent.decision_evidence` (Rust: `antecedent::decision_evidence`) holds several outcomes of one experiment with the covariance of their arm-mean estimators, derives a decision quantity built from them (a margin, say) with a delta-method sampling interval and a separate assumption range, classifies the decision as `robust`, `assumption_dependent` or `unresolved`, and ranks next evidence under a declared planning model. Marginals are never combined into a joint law. See [experimental evidence for decision-relevant outcomes](docs/decision-evidence.md).
- **Categorical choice inference.** `antecedent.choice_inference` (Rust: `antecedent::analysis::choice_inference`) declares a repeated categorical-choice study once and runs the pooled Dirichlet or hierarchical posterior, the stopping decision, the exact operating characteristics of the sequential procedure and an EVSI ranking of candidate studies, with results exported for replay. Stated preferences do not identify behavioural or causal outcomes. See [categorical-choice evidence to decision](docs/choice-evidence.md).
- **Adaptive policy search.** `antecedent.adaptive_policy` searches bounded families of history-dependent rules (a fixed schedule, thresholds on current or lagged observations) with a seeded, replayable derivative-free search, evaluates every candidate on the same draws of a response model, and reports paired contrasts against the schedule baseline with in-sample and held-out values. The result is a `fitted_model_optimization` unless the source states an identification basis. See [optimise history-dependent policies](docs/adaptive-policies.md).
- **Prepare, branch, reuse and embed.** Prepared studies are immutable: `ant.rebind` changes observations, `ant.reconfigure` changes declarations, `branch.reuse` reports what was retained, and every execution returns an `Outcome`. `ant.Execution`, `ant.Frequentist` and `ant.Bayesian` carry execution and inference settings; `ant.estimation.prepare_many` fits a compatible family once.
- **Citations travel with results.** Every result names the provenance records behind the route it executed, with published sources, documented deviations, review scope limits and record digests: `outcome.method_provenance`, `outcome.citations()` and `antecedent.citations.bibliography(...)` (Rust: `StudyResult::method_provenance()`, `antecedent::bibliography`). Exported analysis results carry them in an additive `analysis_result.method_provenance` section, which `ant.load(...).method_provenance` and `.citations()` read back. See [citing the methods behind a study](docs/guides/citing-a-study.md).
- **Data diagnostics.** Automatic, bounded, fit-free diagnostics on prepared tabular and series studies, with configurable suites, explicit opt-out, standalone Rust and Python calls and identity-bound reports. See [data diagnostics](docs/guides/data-diagnostics.md).
- **Composition workflows and bundles with recomputation.** A composed decision is exported as one typed bundle whose nodes include the fits behind it (cyclic posterior, trial-IPW and graph-imputation transport, joint transport, target feedback, assessed bridge posterior, sequential policy reliability and chain decisions). An independent consumer refits and recomputes each node from retained rows under the identity you hold, and a change after export is reported by node. See [compose sources into one decision](docs/composition.md#composition-bundle-and-verifiers) and [choose a composition workflow](docs/guides/composition-workflows.md).
- **Event processes.** `antecedent.events` and the Rust event compiler forecast timestamped arrivals from a finite fixture, a verified provider path or a fixed-decay Hawkes model, drive paired stochastic worlds with the same arrival path and noise, and feed existing decision contracts and same-stream observation studies. See [event forecasts and stochastic decisions](docs/guides/event-processes.md) and [when outside events interrupt a launch](docs/tutorials/event-driven-launch.md).
- **Latent soft interventions.** `antecedent.latent_soft_intervention.prepare` and `Study::prepare_latent_soft_intervention` look for one or two hidden versions of a randomized action on a supplied DAG, report how far the data separate them, and forecast the action as delivered against a baseline at a declared context. See [discover latent soft interventions](docs/guides/latent-soft-interventions.md).
- **Transported and graph-imputed decisions.** Decisions compile from a trial effect reweighted by inverse selection probabilities (trial IPW), from joint transport effects and predictive worlds, and from graph-imputed treatment worlds on binary or mixed-covariate target frames, each with paired action worlds and a replayable identity. Selected treatment links, sequential path contrasts and two assays with a joint error table are covered the same way. See [transported decisions and measurement](docs/guides/transported-decisions-and-measurement.md) and [temporal designs](docs/guides/temporal-designs.md#follow-only-the-treatment-links-you-chose).
- **Weighted target populations.** An `AverageEffect` can target a population declared as row weights or as a kernel over covariate distance (`antecedent.population.target_kernel`, `target_row_weights`; Rust: `WeightedTargetDeclaration`, `StudyBuilder::weighted_target`), and `transport.estimate_trial_effect(..., target=...)` carries a trial effect to such a neighbourhood of the non-participants. The result reports the overlap of the weights with the rows, and a target the data cannot speak for is refused with `transport_target_overlap_failed`. See [ask for the effect in a weighted target population](docs/guides/transport/weighted-target-population.md).
- **Typed mechanism transport.** `antecedent.typed_mechanism_transport` (Rust: `antecedent_model::typed_mechanism_transport::transport_typed`) carries fitted nonlinear and non-Gaussian mechanisms (polynomial or expression means; Gaussian, heteroscedastic, log-normal, Bernoulli, Poisson or gamma responses) to a target population under a selection diagram. Invariant mechanisms come from the source fit and shifted ones only from a target refit; a declared invariance is tested where target data allow. See [carry typed mechanisms into a target model](docs/guides/mechanism-and-synthesis-transport.md#carry-typed-mechanisms-into-a-target-model).
- **Feedback decisions from fitted data.** A joint posterior over fitted feedback mechanisms and target-population disturbances solves each hard-clamp world on the same draw and scores multi-action utility, including a baseline shift of the target population. See [compare actions after fitting the feedback](docs/tutorials/feedback-three-questions.md#compare-actions-after-fitting-the-feedback).
- **Propagation-chain decisions.** A propagation chain read as posterior or predictive retains its aligned output column and row weights; a joint reading retains several columns, and an assumption-envelope reading retains per-draw lower and upper bounds. `chain_decision_v1` and `chain_robust_decision_v1` turn these into nonlinear multi-action and maximin decisions that replay independently. See [producer and consumer matrix](docs/producer-consumer-matrix.md) and [scientific bridges](docs/scientific-bridges.md).
- **Sampling-assumption unions.** Whole-chain assumption envelopes are resampled by IID rows, whole clusters, moving blocks or strata and combined into a Bonferroni union interval that reruns every chain on every resample. See [resample whole groups through the analysis](docs/guides/composed-uncertainty.md#resample-whole-groups-through-the-analysis).
- **Sequential policy reliability.** Aligned raw evaluations of candidate sequential policies compile into a reliability report for the selected policy, and the report can be a node of a bundle. See [check how reliable a sampled or selected decision is](docs/decision-reliability.md) and [evaluate a rule on a separate log](docs/tutorials/programmable-policies.md#evaluate-a-rule-on-a-separate-log).
- **Observation queries.** `antecedent.observation_query` answers what follows from a recorded value: conditioning, intervening, observing after intervening, linear-Gaussian laws, thresholds, evidence required to reach a probability, and parameter uncertainty as aligned draws, with each claim keeping its operation in its identity. See [ask what follows from an observation](docs/observation-queries.md).
- **Missing-outcome sensitivity.** `antecedent.mnar_sensitivity.mnar_ate_sensitivity` and `outcome.value.mnar_sensitivity(...)` (Rust: `mnar_sensitivity_for_study`) re-run a two-arm mean contrast across a declared, bounded missing-not-at-random departure (a delta-adjusted pattern mixture or a bounded selection model) and report where the sign of the effect, the exclusion of the null and each declared decision change. The assumption range, the untestable departure assumption and the per-point sampling interval are kept apart. See [missing outcomes not at random](docs/sensitivity-and-robustness.md#missing-outcomes-not-at-random-mnar_ate_sensitivity).
- **Exposure-measurement corrections.** `antecedent.measurement_exposure` corrects a misclassified exposure (including error that differs by outcome level), per-unit soft exposures, covariate-dependent detection and repeat codings of a binary or ordinal facet. `antecedent.exposure_measurement` declares the same errors on a query's exposure, and `prepare` and `analyze` carry them through the causal estimate by multiple imputation, with a Rubin interval and the naive estimate beside it. See [exposure errors](docs/measurement-models.md#exposure-errors).
- **Development build.** The `development` feature runs routes a release build refuses and records each refusal it would have raised; release builds refuse its artifacts.

### Changed

- **One support rule, and calibration that annotates.** A route is supported when it has provenance and an independent check that currently passes; otherwise it refuses with `not_supported` and lists the missing evidence. `cell_not_licensed` is now `not_supported`, every `*_not_licensed` code is `*_not_supported`, and `claim_license_withdrawn` is `claim_support_withdrawn`. At run time a supported interval is returned with its calibration status, whatever that status is, instead of being refused for want of a coverage record at its coordinate. Whether an interval route is supported is decided by its validation evidence, which for some constructions includes a coverage measurement.
- **Load cheaply; replay on request.** Loading an analysis result checks it is intact and recomputes nothing. `ant.load(..., replay=True)` or `loaded.replay()` (Rust: `load_analysis_result`, `AnalysisResultLoad::replay`) recomputes the published numbers and reports what is not replayable apart from what does not match. Breaking: `consume_analysis_result*` becomes `replay_analysis_result*`; Python acceptance statuses are `loaded`, `replayed` and `unavailable`, and on an analysis result `acceptance.verified`, `.sealed`, `.replayable` and `.unresolved` are removed.
- **Identity and artifact versions.** `IDENTITY_FORMAT` stays 2. Temporal CPDAG and PAG graph identities are versioned inside their own payload (`kind` is `temporal_cpdag.v2` / `temporal_pag.v2`): nodes are sorted and edges re-indexed, so insertion order no longer reaches their digest, and a digest of the earlier encoding is not comparable with it. No other identity digest changes. External response claim metadata is version 2: the provider fingerprint is computed over explicit coordinate fields and counted lists, capabilities are sorted and distinct, and version-1 claims are refused and must be regenerated. Transport proofs, selective-discovery structures, temporal-design, counterfactual-contrast and rollout result sections, and sampled transport artifacts are decoded under stricter checks of the same formats.
- **Artifacts.** Score-table row ids are eight bytes, the scientific-target digest uses payload format 3, decision export is a version 2 envelope that needs the caller's current authority, predictive assessment artifacts are version 2, and mechanism plan identities are encoding version 2. Reduced-retention modes refuse the operations they cannot serve. See [artifact compatibility](docs/migration.md#artifact-compatibility).
- **Minimum supported Rust version.** Building from source or depending on the crates now needs Rust 1.97 (edition 2024); 2.4 built on 1.85. Python support is unchanged (CPython 3.11–3.14).
- **Rust API.** Prepared studies branch with `rebind_data`, `rebind_series`, `rebind_panel` and `rebind_multi_env` (tabular `PreparedStudy::refresh` is removed), physical plans come from the compiler, indexed maps return `MapExecutionError`, execution stamps are read-only, and several retained collections are `RetainedSlice`. See the [Rust table](docs/migration.md#rust).
- **Composition entry points.** `antecedent.events` (`prepare`, `execute`, `count_interval`, `replay`) and `antecedent.latent_soft_intervention` take `execution: Execution | None` in place of loose `memory_bytes`, `cancel` and `seed` arguments, as every other Python entry point does: it carries the memory limit and cancellation, and for latent soft interventions the seed (event seeds stay in the request). Python `NodeKind` and `NODE_KINDS` in `antecedent.composition_bundle` list the thirteen bundle node kinds the features add, so a bundle can reference them and a consumed bundle reports them; they are produced by their fit and decision methods and are not embeddable from bytes. The Rust `design::evsi` facade exports `StudyCostSpec`.

### Fixed

- Answers that were silently wrong now change or refuse: weighted tables on routes that ignored the weights, treatments with undeclared extra levels, declared dependence that a route could not read, multi-treatment derivatives, arrow strength, GES search schedule, composition disjointness inferred from ids, default counterfactual controls and several numeric corrections. See [behaviour changes](docs/migration.md#behaviour-changes-that-can-alter-an-answer-or-turn-it-into-a-refusal).
